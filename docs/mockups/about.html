Our story

A lamplighter’s house, still lit by hand

Lampwright · Dining Rooms · est. 1888

In 1888 the Wharf’s lamplighter turned his front parlour into a supper room for sailors coming off the evening tide. The brass lamps he polished every dusk still hang above table four.

Today Chef Margot Ellery cooks from the same coast and the same walled gardens, with a kitchen that runs on wood, patience and a very old copper stockpot.

We still keep the original ledgers, the brass and the stubborn habit of doing things slowly. What has changed is the cooking: lighter, seasonal and rooted in growers we know by name.

  4. 2014

    Chef Margot

    Margot Ellery takes the pass and rewrites the bill of fare.

The kitchen

Meet Margot Ellery

We cook the way the house is lit: slowly, warmly, and with nothing to hide.

Trained in old harbour kitchens and two seasons abroad, our chef writes the menu each morning with the growers on the phone. The brigade is small, the stockpot is older than any of us, and staff meal is taken seriously.

Margot Ellery

What we believe

House rules

Lit by hand

Every candle and lamp is lit at dusk by the front-of-house team.

Coast & garden

Fish from boats we know by name; vegetables from two walled gardens.

Nothing rushed

Two seatings a night, so your table is yours for the evening.
